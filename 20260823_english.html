TRUST

Keys hold great significance for those who carry them. They represent the trust and authority of the owner when given to another person. We may give our house key to a trusted friend to take care of our matters while we are away. We may give keys to someone responsible for maintaining or working in a particular building. Keys to vehicles are given to people who have proven themselves responsible drivers. The readings today focus on this kind of trust—the trust that conveys authority and responsibility. In the first reading, the symbol is specifically that of a key; but the second reading and the Gospel also speak of trust and leadership as they refer to the earthly church that Jesus established.

Reflecting on the Word

In my catechetical work, as we get close to Holy Week, we meditate on John 12:24: “Unless a grain of wheat shall fall upon the ground and die, it remains but a single grain; but if it dies, it bears much fruit.”

For this meditation, I plant wheat seeds a week apart for three weeks, so that each container has germinated seeds at various stages of growth. I gather the children around me, show them the grain of wheat (they can even hold one in their palm!), and then dig out the plant that was sown last. We observe the appearance and feel of seed—it is different, it isn’t quite as full as the grain in their palm. We do the same for the next two containers. The plant from the third container (sown first) has now lost the husk of the grain. They recognize that the life of the seed is now in the plant. Death has given life in some mysterious way. And more mysteriously, all this has to do with Jesus’ resurrection! One four-year-old then scrunched up his face in concentration and asked with great puzzlement, “Does Jesus really say that dying is a good thing? Why would he say that?” The paradox was too much for him in that moment.

We react similarly to today’s Gospel passage—what does it mean to lose my life and find my life? Why not just hold on to it? The second reading could offer clues to help us decipher the meaning of life and death in Jesus. The image of dying to ourselves in baptism so that we can take on the new risen life of Jesus, so that we may be resurrected at the at the end times, is vivid and gives color to Jesus’ words. We die so that we can rise again. Whether that death constitutes small sacrifices or bodily death, it is so that we can be renewed in body and spirit. Too much for a four-year-old to grasp. They preferred the Circle of Life.
