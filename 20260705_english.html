Fourteenth Sunday in Ordinary Time

July 5, 2026

Come to me, all you who labor and are burdened, and I will give you rest.

— Matthew 11:28

A HUMBLE KING

Like a tonal center in music, the note to which we keep returning in today’s readings is humility. We wait in joyful expectation for the coming of our Lord as King. But what we are told to expect and rejoice over is a meek and humble king, riding on an ass. Not horses or chariots of the great and mighty, but a humble beast carries the Ruler whose dominion stretches “from the River to the ends of the earth” (Zechariah 9:10). This King comes to banish the tools of the warrior and bring peace. No wonder that a weary world rejoices and gives thanks to God!

Reflecting on the Word

“I don’t want to. It would stink!” I couldn’t understand why this little one was starting to get upset. It took me a minute to realize that the child had misunderstood “yoke” as “yolk.” I agreed with him and assured him that Jesus was not talking about an egg but rather a piece of wood that was part of a plow. Although this is not typically a passage that we reflect on with young children, it so happened that this little one was accompanying his older sibling, and partially participated in our reflection. It was funny, but it made me think about how each one of us receives the words of scripture from where we are in our life and context. Hence the misunderstandings and conflicts. The lesson in today’s Gospel and first reading is humility, which is a perfect antidote to conflict. The point that Jesus was making with the yoke analogy was that the complicated requirements of religious observance kept the people from fully experiencing God as love. However, that yoke has now become heavy with the accrual of complicated rules and observances over a history of two thousand years.

Children can show us how to divest ourselves of the encrustations of the centuries and let us go to the very core of our faith, where we can find rest in the arms of our Good Shepherd. We don’t need the peripherals of our faith in order to remain in the covenant with God. May we enjoy the rest and joy that Jesus so deeply desires for us.

Excerpted from Living the Word
