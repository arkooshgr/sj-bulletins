Vocation

Roman Catholics in the United States spend a good deal of time speaking of “vocation” and the manner in which each of us discerns our vocation for life. In today’s apostolic letter, however, Peter gives us the “cornerstone” of our vocation, the description of what each of us is called to first and foremost by our baptism. He describes our vocation as “a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people of [God’s] own, so that you may announce the praises” of Christ, who called us out of darkness into light (1 Peter 2:9). All of us, no matter what path our life’s vocation may take us down, have been chosen by God, made holy, and anointed as priests in the high priesthood of Christ through baptism. All of this, so that we may announce the praises of Christ. Vocation is given for proclamation at home, in the workplace—wherever life takes us— in service, in word, and in sacrament.

Reflecting on the Word

One of my favorite songs is Bernadette Farrell’s Christ, Be Our Light. Each of the five verses has a certain poignancy to it, an intensity of calling us into true discipleship. Today’s second reading from the letter of St. Peter reminds me of the fourth verse:

Longing for shelter, many are homeless. Longing for warmth, many are cold. Make us your building, sheltering others, walls made of living stone.

Peter refers to Jesus as the living stone first, then exhorts us to be like living stones and be built into a “spiritual house.” Farrell takes that verse and makes it much more concrete, connecting it to the shelter that is the basic need of every human person and the responsibility of those with resources to provide for those who are lacking in those resources. The passage from Acts also speaks to the responsibility of the larger community to provide for those in need without discrimination.

In the Gospel, Jesus refers to “dwelling places” in the Father’s house and tells us that we know the way to these dwelling places where we can be with Jesus. The way, which begins at the cross, leads life of Jesus, the risen life that he shares with us in a most special way through this Easter season. The way that leads us toward the one who is longing for shelter and warmth, and the truth that reveals each person’s dignity, finally brings us to the life that animates even stones, so that we can all come together as a community to sing and offer praise to God in song.

— Excerpted from Living the Word
