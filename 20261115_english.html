SHARING OUR GIFTS

Autumn is the season for harvesting and sharing the fruits of our labors. This week’s scriptures use images of the harvest, family life, pregnancy, and investment to describe the abundant harvest in the reign of God. We discover that sharing the gifts we have been given brings eternal rewards for everyone.

In the Gospel parable a master entrusts his possessions to three servants before going on a journey. Each is given some talents. What they do with those talents determines what the master will do with them when he returns. How about us? What are we doing with our talents? How will our Master treat us when he returns?

Reflecting on the Word

I studied Hebrew scriptures under an accomplished and kind rabbi who, in explaining the Sabbath celebration, told the class that he sang the esheyt chayil (“woman of valor”) to his wife every Friday night. As I learned the meaning of this song from the end of the book of Proverbs, I realized that it is not meant to be a portrait of Wonder Woman and induce guilt in those of us who feel, by comparison, inadequate! It is meant to be a song of gratitude, usually sung by her husband at the Sabbath celebration, for all that she is. The word chayil may be interpreted as moral character and resourcefulness. It is generally understood to be a term of high regard. I found myself wishing that my husband would do as the rabbi did. It then occurred to me that every woman longs to be recognized and appreciated for who she is.

So what does the woman of integrity and strength have to do with the servant who traded the money given to him and made more? Or the man who buried it and claimed to have been afraid of his master? One group of children seemed to think that the man who buried the talent was Jesus! As much as I wanted to correct them, I decided to let them explain themselves. The first two servants made more money—but how? Were they honest or did they cheat and exploit others? The one who buried the money refused to be a part of unjust practices to become wealthy. (A talent in Jesus’ time was about twenty years of work for ordinary people, and for someone to have at least eight talents according to this parable is a stunning amount of wealth—like the billionaires of our time.) The master who punishes and ostracizes this person could not represent God for these children who had come to know God as Love. I wholeheartedly agree with the children!

As a woman who seeks the love of God in a community where everyone’s need is fulfilled, I look to the esheyt chayil as inspiration for the fortitude to be that person who cares for everyone with her whole being, just as Jesus does.

Excerpted from Living the Word
