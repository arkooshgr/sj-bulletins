CALL UPON THE LORD

The very first line of today’s first reading summons us to seek the Lord and to call upon God. This sentiment is echoed in the refrain for today’s responsorial psalm: “The Lord is near to all who call upon him” (Psalm 145:18a). Saint Paul is the embodiment of someone who constantly sought the Lord. In the excerpt we read today from his letter to the Philippians, we find Saint Paul toward the end of his life, a life he describes as completely consonant with Christ. He writes, “For to me life is Christ” (Philippians 1:21). To find out what it means to live life completely in accord with Christ we need look no further than today’s Gospel. There we find that God’s love and mercy are immeasurable for all those who seek and call upon the Lord.

Reflecting on the Word

“If I can get paid the whole wage for working only for one hour, I’d rather do that!”

In a catechist training course, I was leading the group of women gathered that Saturday morning in reflecting on the parable Jesus gives us in today’s Gospel. The women began their discussion with trying to receive the most for the least effort.

“Ah, so you’d like to do what the Emperor Constantine did? The deathbed baptism?”

I tried to redirect the conversation: “Why are there some people who are still unemployed at the end of the day? How does that reflect the reality of our world today?

The parable is not so much about philanthropic generosity or about the sacramental graces. Rather, I see Jesus telling us this parable to help us think about how our notions of fairness are not necessarily fair. The verse from Isaiah says clearly that God’s ways are very different from our ways. We tend to forget that in our interpretation of these parables.

The owner of the vineyard could have paid the workers who came first before the others and thus avoided the discontent and complaints of those workers. Instead, he chose to start with the last. I wonder why he did that. What does this parable tell us about how we tend to dismiss, dehumanize, or simply erase from existence by ignoring certain groups of people? But God does not stop inviting humanity into divine love. And God does not stop being patient and kind when we complain and question God’s will for us. God’s ways are not our ways. But we can make our ways more like God’s.
