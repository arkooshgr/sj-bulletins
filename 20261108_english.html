WISDOM

We consider wisdom today. The first reading from the book of Wisdom presents this precious gift as a feminine spirit, a very desirable virtue sought by many and graciously present to all who seek her. In today’s Gospel, Jesus tells a parable to illustrate wisdom in a practical, measurable way. The wise will conserve their resources, use them prudently, and mark the passing of time. The foolish, on the other hand, not planning ahead, will be in the dark. Both of these readings tell us how accessible wisdom is to all who simply and honestly seek it.

Reflecting on the Word

“That is so selfish!” she declared decisively.

The group of eight-year-olds around me expressed their shock and disgust at the apparent selfishness of the wise virgins when they refuse to share the oil for the lamps. The children are also perplexed by the bridegroom’s refusal to let them in, especially if the bridegroom represents God. They have come to know the love of the Good Shepherd and see God as good and merciful. But now, all that they know of God has been questioned and they don’t know what to do with that. We sit and wrestle with the meaning and message of this parable. What is the lamp? What is the oil? How are the virgins both wise and foolish? And so on.

The parables toward the end of Matthew’s Gospel are not easy to decipher and usually leave us squirming. Our first impulse is the reaction of the children, but if we listen carefully to the first reading and the psalm today, we will begin to see clues to help us decipher the parable of the ten virgins, and we can ponder whether the wise virgins did not want to share, or simply could not share the oil. Are they wise because they desire Wisdom?

The group of eight-year-olds wrestled with this, and they decided that none of us can force another to want to be in relationship with God. In that conclusion, they showed their wisdom.

Excerpted from Living the Word
