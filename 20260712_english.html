Fifteenth Sunday in Ordinary Time

July 12, 2026

You have crowned the year with your bounty, and your paths overflow with a rich harvest.

— Psalm 65:12

FRUIT OF THE WORD

The seed is the word of God. The agricultural metaphors in all of today’s readings give richness to the message that the word of God, cultivated in our hearts, bears much fruit in our lives. Creation itself awaits the redemption of all, even as our bodies await the resurrection. Just as we will rejoice in the fulfillment of God’s word, so will all of creation sing for joy in the Reign of God. The word of God is effective and holy; it brings about exactly what it says. It is not futile or empty, but rich in meaning for those who hear and are healed. Those who hear the word of God and keep it will, with all creation, bear much fruit and sing for joy.

Reflecting on the Word

While reflecting on the parable of the sower with seven-year-old children, they thought that the seeds were really God’s love and the soil represented all of humanity. And because we all have free will, they said, we can choose to accept God’s love or to reject it. Ultimately, it does not matter to God if we accept or reject, because God’s love is so abundant and unlimited.

Since this parable has an explanation in Jesus’ voice, I have never tried to think beyond the given analogy that the seeds are the word of God. As I pondered the children’s reflection, I realized that they didn’t really stray from scripture al all when they said that the seeds are the love of God instead of the word of God. God as Love and God as Word are the same. It was deeply moving to me to hear the children say that God would never reject anyone even if they did so themselves. God would keep reaching out until they accepted God’s invitation.

The Isaiah verse assures us that God’s love will never be wasted and will accomplish its intended purpose.

Excerpted from Living the Word
