NOTHING TO FEAR

A rabbi was asked why God sends trials and troubles into human lives. “Because God gets lonely for his people” was the reply. There is some resonance with this wisdom in today’s Gospel, as Jesus makes his disciples get into a boat without him and goes off alone while they venture into stormy waters. Of course, neither Jews nor Christians believe that the Almighty plays this sort of whimsical game with them, but there is some truth in the statement that we don’t turn to God for saving help until we’re in a bind. Peter’s role today, as it is throughout the New Testament, is to be a sign or to represent each member of the church and the whole church. Our story is the tale told about Peter’s faith today. What can bolster our faith is that even when Christ calls us to face bravely the tempests of life, if we are walking through them with our eyes fixed on him in faith, we have nothing to fear. Even when we falter or sink, simply crying “Lord, save me!” is enough to help us know the steady grasp of God’s hand. Then, sheltered inside the walls of faith, the storms we endure lose their power over us.

Reflecting on the Word

“Peter just had to keep his eyes on Jesus. As soon as he became aware of himself, he began to drown.”

“Right. He was too self-conscious.”

The retreatants reflected on this passage from the Gospel. They shook their heads at Peter and his minuscule faith. I had different thoughts in my mind. I wasn’t chiding the disciples for being afraid in the storm or at the sight of the ghost-like figure of Jesus. I wasn’t questioning their faith. I was wondering about Peter saying, “Lord, if it is you.” If? Did he not recognize Jesus’ voice? Did he not recognize the inflection of “It is I”? The first reading shows us how God’s voice comes in the most unexpected, unlikely form. It is not strong wind, or the earthquake, or the fire, but in a gentle whisper. Almost silent. Maybe Peter didn’t really hear Jesus’ voice through the storm. Or maybe Peter’s fear resulted in the lack of trust in Jesus’ words.

If someone had questioned my identity in that way, I would have been much less gracious than Jesus was at that moment. I was also struck by the overwhelming fear inundating all but Jesus. The storm, the figure, the drowning...so many moments of fear. And doubt. It is that doubt about Jesus’ very person that Peter is admonished for (I think). Maybe Peter should’ve kept his eyes and ears on Jesus and he wouldn’t have had to step out of the boat at all. Jesus would have come into his boat soon enough.
