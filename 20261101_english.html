LIVING AND PRAYING WITH THE WORD

O God of honor, power, and might! You show us in the life of Jesus that your power and might are not what humanity considers power and might, but that it is in walking on the other side of power and might that we enter into your eternal glory. Grant us, we pray, the clarity of heart and vision to see you in those around us as we strive to make ourselves pure and holy as you are holy. Amen.

Reflecting on the Word

Growing up outside of the U.S.A., the observation of All Saints Day and All Souls Day were twin celebrations. As we prayed to those who have been successful in their effort to “be holy as [God is] holy” in the span of their earthly life, we also prayed for those whose efforts were still ongoing beyond their death. These twin celebrations expressed our belief that the latter would eventually join the former and that both groups are models for us in our efforts to “be holy as [God is] holy.” Jesus names the ones who are blessed because they emulate the God who is love. The path to holiness is not a bullet-point list that we can check off but a journey as we push the boundaries of our comfort and complacency. The blood of the Lamb that washes us is also the blood of our fellow human persons that could be transformed by our willingness to walk with them without judgment and purity, so that we may rejoice in their becoming children of God. As incomprehensible as this Gospel might be to some of us, as difficult as it might be to be pure and meek, we remember that God is ahead of us to help us be successful in our endeavor to become a saint, a holy one. The blessings that Jesus promises to us on this journey go before us, empowering us, and follow after us, marking us as God’s beloved children. We shall all one day see the glory of God.

Excerpted from Living the Word
