GOD’S WAYS

In the stream of readings during this portion of Ordinary Time one week’s texts can often appear to pick up exactly where the previous week’s concluded. The sentiments expressed in today’s first reading seem to be a direct reaction to the Gospel passage we heard last week. In that Gospel, the landowner pays the same wages to his workers whether they worked for a full day or for only a few minutes. Today Ezekiel gives us the lament, “The LORD’s way is not fair!” (Ezekiel 18:25). These Sundays in Ordinary Time offer us a glimpse into the ways of the Lord. We see how God’s way has the tendency to turn the accepted conventions of the day upside down. Those who always expected that their way to heaven was guaranteed are disappointed. Those who thought they never had a chance are given that chance. Today offers us another opportunity to discover the abundance of God’s mercy and love.

Reflecting on the Word

This is one of those parables that gets interesting when we put ourselves in the place of the religious leaders and listen to Jesus telling us this parable. Which one of the two sons would we like to be? It is uncomfortable to ask ourselves which son we most resemble. Ah yes, we would like to be the son who does his father’s bidding rather than the son who speaks the right words but does not act on it. But we could very well be the other one. In reality, neither option is flattering.

Let us say that the disobedient son depicts a Christian, and the other son depicts those who don’t believe in Jesus or his salvific gift to humanity. Or even those who don’t believe in God at all. Will they be part of God’s reign?

Growing up in a cosmopolitan city where I encountered people of different religious affiliations, I saw how their actions often appeared to live the gospel with little effort, even though they were not Christian, and I could not fathom how God would relegate them to the eternal fire just because they had inherited and chose to remain in a religion other than Christianity. Like the son who does as his father bids him, these people did the good works of mercy. I wonder if this parable directs us to live in great humility and faithfulness as we expand our understanding of the Kin-dom of God. We all, in the meantime, need to keep living faithfully and leave the rest up to God!
